FarmHub Agro

FarmHub Agro Services is a food business in Osogbo, Nigeria, that grew from a farm-produce outlet into a full supermarket. I designed its customer app, where people order food and set up regular deliveries, and the staff dashboard that runs the app behind the scenes: prices, stock, orders, riders and reports. I also built the front end of both.

Design

FarmHub had grown from three farm products into a supermarket, but the way customers ordered had not changed. The order form on its website listed three products and one delivery option, so most customers sent a WhatsApp message asking whether an item was in stock and how much it cost. Staff answered those messages one at a time, checked bank transfers by hand, and had no single place to see orders, deliveries or stock. I mapped these problems and the people behind them before designing anything, and they shaped every product that followed.

01

What shaped the product

Four findings shaped the design of the app and the dashboard.

  • Prices that staff can update themselves

    Prices change often in a Nigerian food market, and a catalogue that needs a developer to update it quickly stops being trusted. Staff change a price once in the dashboard, and the app charges that same price.

  • Ordering as easy as sending a message

    Customers were used to ordering by chat, so the app had to be at least as quick. It keeps ordering to a few taps: sizes are chosen from cards, the basket stays on the phone, and checkout only asks what applies.

  • Two very different delivery zones

    Orders inside Osogbo go out by rider the same day, while orders elsewhere in Nigeria go by courier and are paid in advance. Checkout shows each area with its fee and asks only the questions that apply to its zone.

  • Made for mid-range phones and mobile data

    Most customers shop on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data. Pages stay light, with no animation library and no carousels that move by themselves, and searching hundreds of items stays quick.

02

Who it’s for

FarmHub serves the customers buying food and the team who fulfil their orders. They share one goal, an order delivered correctly and on time, but each group needs something different. I grouped them into four types of users.

  • Customers

    Households and small businesses in and around Osogbo, usually on a mid-range Android phone and mobile data.

    Needs to

    • Find an item and its price quickly
    • Order without a long chat
    • Know when the order will arrive
    • Get regular deliveries without reordering

  • Managers

    Run the business and need a clear picture of how it is doing.

    Needs to

    • Compare sales with last week and last month
    • See the best sellers
    • Follow subscriptions
    • Spot anything waiting for attention

  • Store staff

    Sales, dispatch and inventory staff, each responsible for one part of the work.

    Needs to

    • Confirm paid orders and check transfers
    • Send orders out with the right rider
    • Keep stock and prices up to date

    Each role starts on its own home screen in the dashboard.

  • Riders

    Deliver orders across Osogbo, using the dashboard on a phone outdoors.

    Needs to

    • See only their own deliveries
    • Call the customer in one tap
    • Mark each step of a delivery

03

The principles behind every decision

I set these five rules at the start and used them to settle decisions across all three products.

  1. 1

    Keep the brand people already know

    The client asked for the existing site’s standard or better, not a new look. All three products use the greens and type of farmhubagro.com.

  2. 2

    Write every status in words

    Out of stock is written in words, and a falling figure shows in amber with the word “Down” instead of red against green.

  3. 3

    Start people on their own work

    Each staff role opens the dashboard on the work it does, instead of a page of figures it cannot act on.

  4. 4

    Stay light on mobile data

    No animation library, no self-moving carousels and no images the page doesn’t need, because customers pay for every megabyte.

  5. 5

    Be honest about what is done by hand

    A bank transfer is confirmed by a member of staff, so an order paid that way says it is waiting for payment until they do.

05

Key screens in the customer app

The app is where customers shop with FarmHub, from finding an item to getting the same groceries every week. These are its key screens, in the order a customer meets them.

Finding food and its price

The home screen opens on search, categories and everyday staples, so a customer can find rice or palm oil without knowing where it sits in the catalogue. Product photos from FarmHub’s catalogue sit on a sage background, so every item looks consistent.

On a product page, every size is a card with its own price, so nobody has to guess what the bigger bag costs.

A basket that stays on the phone, and a short checkout

The basket bar follows the customer from screen to screen with the item count and the total. The basket is kept on the phone, so it is still there when they come back to it.

Checkout lists the areas inside Osogbo, delivered the same day, apart from places elsewhere in Nigeria, which are paid in advance. Every area shows its fee before the customer chooses it.

Following an order to the door

An order moves through five steps, from placed to delivered, and each step is written in words as well as marked with a dot. The customer also sees which rider is bringing it and the address it is going to.

When it arrives, the same screen shows the order as delivered, with a way back to the shop and to past orders.

Regular deliveries without reordering

A household that buys the same things every week can turn its basket into a regular delivery. The customer picks a plan, a day and how many deliveries to keep it for, and can pause or cancel at any time from their account.

The subscription screen shows the next delivery, what is in each box and the card it is charged to. After the last delivery, FarmHub suggests a fresh box based on what the household buys.

06

The rider’s phone

Riders deliver orders across Osogbo and use the dashboard on their own phones, outdoors and on the move. Their view leaves out everything except the deliveries in front of them.

Today’s deliveries, one tap from the customer

A rider signs in to a home screen built around today’s deliveries. Each delivery shows the address, a button to call the customer and a way to mark each step.

Every step a rider marks also updates the order in the customer’s app, which is how the customer knows who is bringing it and when it has been delivered.

07

The dashboard: the control centre behind the app

Everything a customer sees in the app is run from the staff dashboard. Staff set prices and stock, change what the app shows, follow customers and what they buy, and see how the business is doing.

The overview: how the business is doing, at a glance

Managers wanted to know how today compared with the same point last week and last month. Instead of a row of identical stat cards, I put today, this week and this month in one deep-green band that reads from left to right, each figure with its change written in words.

Below it sit a 30-day sales chart, subscriptions that need attention, best sellers and sales by category.

Prices and stock that the app follows

Every price on the products page is what the shop and the app charge, so staff change a price once and customers see the new one. Each product shows how many are left and whether it is on sale, with one menu to change its price or stock.

Staff change the app themselves

The announcement line, the home-screen banners and where each banner leads are all edited from the dashboard, and changes show in the app within five minutes. Nobody needs a developer to announce a public holiday or start a promotion.

Customers and what they buy

Each customer has a page with their details, whether they have an app account and everything they have bought, most often first. That history is what a regular delivery box is built from, and staff can set up a subscription or adjust loyalty points from the same page.

Reports for any period

Reports cover sales, profit on goods, promotion results, how many app visitors went on to buy and which baskets were left unpaid. Each report can be printed or downloaded for the accountant.

A home screen for each staff role

A dispatcher has no use for a sales chart. Dispatch staff open the dashboard on what is ready to go out, what each rider is carrying and which deliveries are running late. Sales, inventory and rider staff each get their own home in the same way.

08

The design system

All three products share one set of design tokens taken from the live farmhubagro.com site, so they read as one brand. The tokens live in a single file and are generated into the website, the app and the dashboard, so a colour changed once changes everywhere. The light green is only ever used for decoration because it is too faint to read as text.

  • Green #2a7d2e buttons and links
  • Deep green #17511a headings and the figures band
  • Light green #94d96b decoration only, 1.9:1 on white
  • Paper #fbfaf7 page background
  • Ink #1e2227 text
  • Amber #8a6100 warnings, always with a word

Typeface

Aa

Tenor Sans + Public Sans

Tenor Sans, the serif-like face from the existing site, sets headings and figures. Public Sans sets the interface text in the app and the dashboard because it stays clear at small sizes.

Standards every screen meets

  • Text contrast of at least 4.5:1 on every colour pair
  • A visible focus ring on every control
  • Tap targets of at least 44px on phones
  • Status written in words, never shown by colour alone
  • Motion only in CSS, and none when reduced motion is turned on

Development

I built the front end of all three products. The website order pages are a WordPress plugin written in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework, so it runs on the existing site and stays fast on Nigerian mobile networks. The customer app is built with Expo and React Native in TypeScript, and runs on iPhone, Android and the web from one codebase. The staff dashboard is built with Laravel and Filament, where I built the theme, the pages and the home screen for each role.

Three front ends, one set of design tokens.

03

Design tokens the code won’t let you misuse

The light brand green is too faint to read as text, so the app’s colour tokens carry a type that says how each one may be used. Using a decoration colour for text stops the app from compiling, so the mistake is caught before it can reach a customer.

The same tokens are generated into the website’s CSS and the dashboard’s theme, and an automatic check fails if any copy drifts from the source.

04

One dashboard, a different home for each role

When someone signs in, the dashboard picks their home screen from their most senior role. Managers see the business, and everyone else starts on the work their role does. Someone with no role yet sees only a greeting rather than figures they shouldn’t see.

05

A basket that survives the app closing

People often shop in pieces, adding a few items now and finishing later. The basket is saved on the phone every time it changes and restored when the app opens again, so nothing is lost if the app closes or the network drops.

06

An order form with no framework

The website order form is plain JavaScript, so it loads quickly on mobile data and keeps working through theme updates. When the customer changes delivery zone, the payment options change with it, and switching back restores the default so nobody is asked for a receipt they never meant to attach.

07

Testing and standards

Checks that run on every change to the front ends, and the standards behind them.

  • Contrast

    Every one of the 16 colour pairs in the tokens is checked against WCAG 2.2 AA.

  • Token sync

    Fails if the website, app or dashboard copy of the tokens drifts from the source.

  • App logic

    Tests for the basket, prices, search and money handling in the customer app.

  • Keyboard and focus

    A visible focus ring on every control, and the whole order form works by keyboard.
