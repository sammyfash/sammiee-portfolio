BMONI

BMONI lets people and businesses hold, swap, spend and send money in several currencies from one app, through licensed financial partners. I designed and built its three websites: bmoni.com, the global front door, a site for customers in Nigeria, and a bilingual site for businesses in Mexico.

Design

BMONI launched in Nigeria for individuals, freelancers and families, then in Mexico for businesses that run payroll and pay suppliers abroad. The two markets have different customers, a different language and a different offer, but both had to feel like the same trustworthy company. BMONI is also not a bank, so before anyone downloads the app the website has to explain plainly who holds their money and how it is protected. I designed one system that could carry all of that, then used it to build a full site for each market and a single screen at bmoni.com that sends every visitor to the right one.

01

What shaped the sites

Four problems decided how the three sites are split and what each page has to say.

  • Two markets with very different customers

    In Nigeria, BMONI serves people: freelancers paid in dollars, families supporting relatives abroad, travellers. In Mexico it serves businesses running payroll, paying suppliers and issuing tax invoices. One page could not speak to both, so each market has its own site, its own offer and its own call to action.

  • Trust comes before the download

    People are being asked to keep their money in an app that is not a bank. Every site says so in plain words, names the licensed partners behind the service, explains the facial recognition used for every login and payment, and gives security a page of its own.

  • Visitors don’t know which BMONI they need

    Someone arriving from an ad or a search may not know that BMONI runs a separate service in each country. bmoni.com is one screen with a map of where BMONI is live and where it is coming next, and a button for each market.

  • Mexican businesses read in Spanish

    A finance manager in Mexico expects to read about payroll, SPEI transfers and CFDI invoices in Spanish. bmoni.mx opens in Spanish with English one click away, and search engines are told which language and country each page is for.

02

Who it’s for

BMONI is for people and businesses whose money crosses borders. They all want the same thing, moving money quickly and cheaply without worrying about it, but each arrives with a different job to do.

  • Freelancers and remote workers

    Paid by clients abroad and want to keep their earnings in dollars.

    Needs to

    • Open NGN and USD virtual accounts
    • Swap currencies in seconds
    • Spend online with a virtual card

  • Families and students abroad

    Support someone in another country, or study away from home.

    Needs to

    • Send money to bank accounts abroad
    • Manage money in six currencies
    • Know the money is protected

  • Businesses in Mexico

    Run payroll and pay suppliers in more than one currency.

    Needs to

    • Hold MXN, USD and EUR in one place
    • Pay payroll, suppliers and SAT obligations
    • Book a demo with the BMONI team

  • Travellers

    Want to spend abroad without carrying cash or paying high fees.

    Needs to

    • Use a virtual or physical Mastercard
    • Pay at over 100 million merchants
    • Top up a USD wallet by bank transfer or stablecoin

03

The principles behind every decision

I set these at the start, and they settled most decisions across all three sites.

  1. 1

    One brand, many markets

    Every market uses the same colours, type, buttons and sections. A new market changes its words, photos and offer, not the design, so BMONI looks like one company wherever you meet it.

  2. 2

    Show the real product

    The pages use real app screens and the real cards instead of abstract illustrations, so visitors can see exactly what they would be using before they download it.

  3. 3

    Say who holds the money

    Every site states that BMONI is a financial technology company, not a bank, and names the licensed partners that provide the banking, in the security page, the FAQ and the footer.

  4. 4

    One clear action per market

    In Nigeria every page ends with Download Now. In Mexico, where the customer is a business, every page ends with a request for a demo.

  5. 5

    Show the people BMONI serves

    The photography shows the customers in each market, young Nigerians in Lagos and business owners in Mexico, so visitors can picture themselves using it.

05

Key pages across the three sites

The pages that explain BMONI, earn a visitor’s trust and ask them to act, in each market.

One front door for every market

bmoni.com is a single screen. It says what BMONI is, shows a map of where it is live and where it is coming next, and gives each market its own button. When BMONI launches somewhere new, the map gains a live pin and the page gains a button.

Products explained in everyday terms

The Nigeria products page takes the app one feature at a time: wallets in six currencies, virtual and physical cards, currency conversion in seconds and transfers abroad. Each feature is described by what it lets you do, next to a real screen from the app.

A page for each kind of customer

Use Cases speaks to each customer directly: salaries paid into dollar wallets, freelancers with clients in London, families supporting someone abroad, travellers spending at over 100 million merchants. Visitors find their own situation instead of a generic feature list.

Security as a page of its own

Because BMONI is not a bank, security gets its own page. It explains the facial recognition behind every login and payment, the 22 patents behind it, and the licensed partners that hold and move the money.

Mexico: a treasury platform, in Spanish

The Mexico homepage speaks to businesses. It opens with the BMONI Business dashboard and the card, describes treasury in MXN, USD and EUR, payroll and supplier payments, and asks for a demo instead of a download.

Solutions for Mexican companies

Soluciones covers what a finance team needs day to day: wallets with their own account details, SPEI transfers, CFDI invoices that comply with the SAT, payroll and business cards, all from one dashboard.

Development

All three sites run on WordPress with Elementor Pro, on the Hello Elementor theme. They share one set of global colours, type and sections, and each market is its own WordPress site on its own domain, so Nigeria and Mexico can change their offer, language and news without touching each other.

03

Telling search engines which site is which

Three domains and two languages could easily compete with each other in search results. Every page declares its alternatives, so Google sends Nigerians to bmoni.ng, Spanish speakers in Mexico to bmoni.mx, English speakers in Mexico to its English pages, and everyone else to bmoni.com.

04

One set of global styles

Colours and type are Elementor global values, not settings on each widget. Every heading, button and section reads from them, so a change to the brand is made once and reaches every page on every site.

05

Spanish first, English one click away

In Mexico the Spanish site lives at the root of bmoni.mx and the English version under /mx/, with a switch in the header. Both use the same templates, so a new section is designed once and translated, not rebuilt.
